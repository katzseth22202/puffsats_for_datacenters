\title{PuffSat Pulsed Propulsion}
\subtitle{A Speculative Application to Space Data Centers}
A holy grail for space propulsion is a rocket that offers high specific impulse and sufficient thrust to reach orbit.   Pulsed propulsion, like Project Orion and its successors, theoretically provides this.  It failed because it produces fallout, risks proliferating mass produced nuclear bombs, and can't easily be scaled down to small explosions.  A satellite launched from Earth can use a Jupiter gravity assist to return to Earth on a retrograde trajectory at speeds up to \siunitx{70}{\kilo\meter\per\second}.  Recent progress on tight satellite formations means we can aim a target rocket at a sequence of precisely times satellites and use their energy with a pusher plate, like Orion.  These satellites, which we'll call PuffSats, explode to gas before hitting the plate rather than pulverize it.   If we mix this exploding gas with gas sprayed in front of the plate by the target rocket, we can launch even greater mass to orbit for a given PuffSat gas mass.   We can also mix an incoming impactor with a significantly larger amount of mass inside a de Laval nozzle and use the resulting fireball to move in the direction the impactor came from, even though the impactor's momentum cancel some of the rocket's momentum.

We briefly describe how to use this idea with a focus on a motivating example of pushing data centers into orbit.  We will also argue these data centers should be put in high orbits or Lagrange points rather than sun synchronous orbits.   Unfortunately, Iran has demonstrated data centers will be targeted in combat.   For our system to be competitive with fully reusable rockets it must therefore match the cost of launching mass to these high orbits.   We assume this implies initial costs of \$400 \si{\per\kilo\gram} and speculate on how we would reach this price.   This document is a narrower more focused look at the idea, which is much more exhaustively considered in the companion white paper \textit{Aim Is All You Need}~\cite{katz2026aim}.  That paper is a pretty long read, less well vetted, and was initially focused on laying the groundwork for hard science fiction, whereas this paper is intended to provide a more focused plausible initial look at the technology using data centers as a motivating example.
