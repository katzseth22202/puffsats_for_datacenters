\begin{abstract}\label{sec:abstract}
% Elevator pitch. The hook, then just enough context to know what the paper is.
% The siting argument, its citations, and the cost case belong in the body.
% See docs/brief_guide.md and CONTEXT.md.
High-thrust, high-specific-impulse propulsion is a long-standing goal of rocket design. Nuclear-pulse concepts such as Project Orion~\cite{projorion} showed in principle how externally supplied, high-energy pulses could shatter the limits of chemical rockets, but at the unacceptable cost of using nuclear explosives.

This paper introduces \textit{PuffSat Pulsed Propulsion}, a non-nuclear analogue of Orion in which small satellites, dubbed PuffSats, are sent on Jupiter gravity-assist trajectories that return them to Earth at \SIrange{55}{68}{\kilo\meter\per\second}, corresponding to \SIrange{1.5}{2.3}{\giga\joule\per\kilogram} of kinetic energy. The PuffSats complete the Earth–Jupiter–Earth round trip in roughly \SIrange{2.2}{3.3}{\yr}.  Shortly before intercepting the target vehicle, each PuffSat disperses into gas, which strikes a pusher plate. Additional propellant carried by the target vehicle is injected into and mixed with the resulting plasma, increasing the momentum transferred per kilogram of returning PuffSat mass. Alternatively, the incoming material can mix with injected propellant inside a pulsed nozzle chamber, allowing the resulting expansion to produce directed thrust, including thrust toward the oncoming PuffSat.

We develop one candidate PuffSat architecture and evaluate its performance. As a motivating application, we consider the launch of large orbital data centers and examine whether such a system could achieve launch costs competitive with near-term fully reusable launch vehicles such as SpaceX Starship~\cite{starship}. The companion paper \textit{Aim Is All You Need}~\cite{katz2026aim} explores the broader architectural implications.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Objects crossing Earth on retrograde heliocentric orbits can carry specific kinetic energies hundreds of times the chemical energy per unit mass of rocket propellants. \textit{PuffSat Pulsed Propulsion} seeks to convert this kinetic energy into high-specific-impulse, high-thrust propulsion. The concept builds on ideas from Project Orion and pellet-beam propulsion. Unlike most pellet-beam concepts (see \cite{katz2026aim}), PuffSats rely on celestial mechanics rather than hypothetical giant accelerators or nuclear explosions to achieve high speeds.

Large orbital data centers are a potential near-term market for very heavy lift. We compare speculative early PuffSat launch costs with those of a fully reusable rocket system serving the same destinations (see \autoref{sec:launch_cost_speculation}). If returning PuffSats can deliver enough momentum to launch a greater mass of replacement PuffSats onto new Jupiter trajectories, the available returning mass can grow exponentially from cycle to cycle (\autoref{sec:exponential_mass_growth}). Once bootstrapped, this cycle could supply its own reaction mass without repeatedly launching that mass aboard an orbital-class reusable rocket. The initial conventional launch cost would therefore become progressively less important to the system's overall cost.

PuffSat Pulsed Propulsion would build on advances in autonomous satellite formation flying. PRISMA demonstrated autonomous relative navigation and control in orbit~\cite{damico2011prisma_safe}, while the VISORS mission develops these capabilities for a precision CubeSat formation~\cite{guffanti2023autonomous}. PuffSat formations would be much larger, although their formation-keeping requirements would be less precise than those of science missions such as VISORS. PuffSats must also solve the distinct problem of hit-to-target guidance between the PuffSat swarm and the receiving vehicle, together with target-vehicle corrections between successive impacts (see \autoref{sec:guidance_navigation}).

Each PuffSat must carry a low-cost gas-forming payload through a long interplanetary trajectory and then release a plume shaped for useful momentum and energy transfer. Its electronics and other dry mass must safely miss the receiving vehicle. We describe a candidate spin-stabilized satellite in \autoref{sec:puffsat_satellite_architecture} and the required trajectories in \autoref{sec:jupiter_gravity_assists}. At the receiving vehicle, a pusher plate or pulsed nozzle must convert the arriving flow into thrust. The efficiency of either approach depends on shock formation, chemical and thermal losses, and material survivability (see \autoref{sec:pusher_plates} and \autoref{sec:rocket_nozzles}).

The companion white paper was originally written to establish the technical groundwork for hard-science-fiction world-building. This paper asks the narrower question of whether a focused application of PuffSat Pulsed Propulsion could make sense for real-world space missions. Its purpose is to expose the concept and its assumptions to critical analysis and to solicit feedback from experts in the relevant fields.

\section{Data Centers, Anti-Satellite Weapons, And Launch Costs}\label{sec:data_center_attacks}
For the data-center application, the launch-cost comparison depends on the destination, which must
satisfy both latency and survivability requirements.
Iran's drone strikes on Amazon's Gulf data centers in March 2026 established that data centers are
wartime targets~\cite{cnbc2026_aws_strike,gatech2026_aws_strike}. Those were drones against
buildings, so they say nothing about orbital reach. China's destruction of the FY-1C weather
satellite at \SI{865}{\kilo\meter} in January 2007 says a great deal~\cite{kelso2007asat,nasa_odqn2007_fengyun}.
A direct-ascent interceptor~\cite{kan2007_asat} arrives at sun-synchronous altitude in minutes, which leaves no window to
disperse a cluster or to engage the inbound vehicle. A transfer to an Earth-Moon Lagrange point takes
days, at a cost of \SI{2.6}{\second} of round-trip light time to an Earth customer. Distance does not
hide a data center, and a published halo orbit is as predictable as a sun-synchronous one. What
distance buys is the time an active response needs.
