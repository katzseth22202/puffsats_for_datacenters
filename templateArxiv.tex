\begin{abstract}\label{sec:abstract}
% Elevator pitch. The hook, then just enough context to know what the paper is.
% The siting argument, its citations, and the cost case belong in the body.
% See docs/brief_guide.md and CONTEXT.md.
High-thrust, high-specific-impulse propulsion is the holy grail for rocket engines. Nuclear-pulse concepts such as Project Orion~\cite{projorion} demonstrated that externally supplied, high-energy pulses can break the chemical-propulsion tradeoff, but at the unacceptable cost of nuclear explosives.

This paper introduces \textit{PuffSat Pulsed Propulsion}, a non-nuclear analogue of Orion that uses small satellites dubbed PuffSats sent on Jupiter-assist trajectories and returned to Earth at \SIrange{55}{68}{\kilo\meter\per\second}, carrying \SIrange{1.5}{2.3}{\giga\joule\per\kilogram} of kinetic energy. Shortly before intercept, each PuffSat disperses into gas that impacts a pusher plate, where additional onboard propellant is injected and mixed with the incoming plasma, amplifying momentum transfer. Alternatively, the fireball mixes in a pulsed nozzle chamber to provide directional thrust, including in the head-on direction.

We apply one proposed PuffSat architecture. As a motivating application, we evaluate a design focused on launching large orbital data centers with launch costs that could be competitive with near-term reusable launch systems like SpaceX Starship~\cite{starship}.

Broader architectural implications are explored in the companion paper \textit{Aim Is All You Need}~\cite{katz2026aim}.
\end{abstract}
